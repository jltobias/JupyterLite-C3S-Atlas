# Explore climate. Inspect the evidence.

![Original illustrated climate globe; decorative artwork, not a data map](assets/atlas-splash.png)

**A browser-based field guide to the Copernicus Interactive Climate Atlas.**
Learn to ask climate questions, inspect source data, calculate carefully, and
communicate results with appropriate uncertainty.

and [new learning route](pulse-guide.md). Export regional GeoJSON for RustGIS and
GeoLibre. This brings the course to **29 executable Python notebooks**.

## Two ways to learn

Read the executed chapters here, or open the same notebooks in JupyterLite Lab to
change the code on your own device. Each chapter has an original splash illustration,
a guiding question, working calculations, an interpretation check, a transfer task
in the official Atlas, and source credits. Most chapters take 20–35 minutes.

The first browser visit needs internet for Python and packages. Lab saves in browser
storage, not GitHub: download work you want to keep. See the [data guide](data-guide.md).

## Follow the questions

| Route | Chapters | You will learn to… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read the evidence | 1–4 | Identify data, preserve provenance, choose baselines, and interpret seasons. |
| Explore the climate system | 5–10 | Separate means, counts, extremes, water balance, energy context, and cryosphere/ocean signals. |
| Compare possible futures | 11–14 | Interpret ensembles, real warming-level windows, uncertainty, bias adjustment, and spatial weights. |
| Make the patterns visible | 15–18 | Compare 2D maps, rotating globes, 3D scenes, monitoring, and urban context. |
| Build a reproducible story | 19–20 | Import authentic C3S data and write a bounded, cited evidence card. |
| Monitor the Pacific | 21–25 | Audit real Pulse data, locate Niño regions, compare relative indices, and calculate transparent ranks. |
| Share the evidence | 26–29 | Explore region maps/globes, global context, GIS exports, and a reproducible storymap. |

## Know what you are looking at

**Synthetic teaching examples** explain methods and appear with explicit labels.
They are not climate observations, forecasts, or official Atlas outputs.
**Real bundled references** include the upstream CMIP6 warming-level table, a CDS
catalogue snapshot, and public-domain coastlines. **Your real C3S subset** can be
added through the documented import route. The full gridded archive is not bundled.
**Real Pulse CSVs** in the new chapters retain their CC BY 4.0 headers and dated
snapshot hashes. Their regional averages are not SST pixels or forecasts.

This independent project complements the
[official C3S Atlas](https://atlas.climate.copernicus.eu/) and
[official user-tools book](https://ecmwf-projects.github.io/c3s-atlas/).
Consult the [variable coverage index](variables.md), [training links](learning-resources.md),
and [citations and attribution](credits.md).
