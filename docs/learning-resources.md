## A workshop route

**First session:** orientation, data provenance, baselines, and seasonal cycles.
Predict a plot before running it; then describe its axes, units, reference period,
and the limits of the evidence.

**Second session:** follow heat, water, drought, energy, snow/ice, ocean, and circulation
themes. Compare a mean, an extreme, and a threshold count. Discuss which questions
monthly data cannot answer.

**Third session:** compare scenarios, warming levels, model spread, map projections,
regional averages, and three-dimensional displays. Rotate a globe and compare it
with a flat map; discuss what the extra dimension reveals and conceals.

**Capstone:** use an authentic Atlas export, document its licence and provenance,
and prepare an evidence card containing one question, one figure, one result,
one uncertainty statement, and one limitation.

## Media and accessibility

The official user guide links to the Atlas introductory video. Open videos at the
publisher so their captions, source credits, and usage terms remain available.
The lessons do not require watching videos. Every exercise has written instructions,
and interactive displays have data tables or static alternatives. Avoid inferring
values from colour alone: read the legend and inspect numerical values.
## Climate Pulse and open GIS learning route

Start with the [nine new chapters](pulse-guide.md),
[interactive Pulse lab](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-C3S-Atlas/pulse/), and
[ENSO storymap](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-C3S-Atlas/stories/enso/).
For a 90-minute workshop, combine source auditing (21), relative ENSO (24), and
the GIS export lab (28). For a longer course, follow all chapters 21–29.

- [Official Climate Pulse and its in-app tutorial videos](https://pulse.climate.copernicus.eu/)

explanations and executable alternatives. No publisher media are copied.
## Official-app workshops

[Chapters 30–32](apps-guide.md) add global Pulse comparisons, an explicit CSV
download/upload audit, and an 11-app catalogue investigation. Explore
[official Pulse](https://pulse.climate.copernicus.eu/) and the
[Copernicus applications catalogue](https://apps.climate.copernicus.eu/).
Use [ERA Explorer's official notebooks](https://apps.copernicus-climate.eu/c3s-apps/era-explorer/notebooks/intro.html)
and the [Weather Replay introduction/video](https://climate.copernicus.eu/weather-replay-your-time-machine-revisit-past-weather)
